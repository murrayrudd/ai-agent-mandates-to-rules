You can obtain one specified API data response from the same seller under offer A or B, or decline. The two offers buy identical data, quality, usage rights, and delivery timing. All displayed money amounts are US cents. Accounts and credentials are ready; the quoted fee covers all payment costs. One cent is one hundredth of a US dollar.

On each purchase you have a separate allocation of 2,500 cents. The nonrefundable fee is added to the service price of 100 cents. Their sum must be at most 130 cents. A separate 2,000-cent reserve is locked for 24 hours and has no carrying cost in this exercise. The remaining uncommitted cash is an accounting balance. There is no borrowing, replacement purchase, or second attempt.

Payment succeeds with either offer. Five seconds later the seller delivers usable data with probability 99%, or confirms non-delivery with probability 1%. After non-delivery a separate guarantee returns the stated purchase-price reimbursement after 24 hours. Independently, a reserve incident with the displayed probability permanently removes 1,000 cents from the reserve. The rest returns at the end of the commitment. The reserve-loss probability already accounts for the payment arrangement.

Operator-held keys let the operator release the reserve without a provider signature. Provider-held keys require the provider’s signature. Routine release time and costs are the same. A transaction pseudonym does not link purchases, a persistent pseudonym links purchases to a reusable identifier, and verified legal identity links purchases to a name. In each case the payment record lasts 30 days on otherwise identical terms. Declining gives zero value, costs nothing, and ends this opportunity.
